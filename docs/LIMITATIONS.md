# AAGAM — Operational Limitations and Disclaimers

**Project:** Adaptive AI-Grid Assimilation Model (AAGAM)  
**Hackathon / Problem Statement:** Smart India Hackathon 2026 — PS 26081 (MoES / NCMRWF)  
**Document Type:** Technical & Operational Limitations Specification (PRD §18, §19)  
**Phase:** Phase 9 Hardening & Evaluation  

---

## 1. Geographical & Spatial Coverage

- **40 Representative Synoptic Stations:**  
  AAGAM's current operational demonstration operates over 40 carefully selected synoptic stations and district headquarters across 7 distinct geographical zones in India (North, Northwest, Central, South, East, Northeast, and Islands).
- **Not a Full Continuous National Grid:**  
  While the backend architecture is designed to scale horizontally to a continuous 0.25° $\times$ 0.25° nationwide grid (~5,000 land points), the prototype deployment is intentionally configured for the 40 core synoptic stations to ensure predictable API latency, efficient compute consumption, and adherence to cloud hosting resource limits.
- **Topographical Microclimates:**  
  Stations situated in complex mountainous terrain (e.g., Shimla, Srinagar, Leh, Gangtok) exhibit localized microclimates that may deviate from regional point forecasts due to sub-grid topographic variance not fully captured by 0.25° global NWP models.

## 2. Regulatory & Advisory Status Disclaimer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **AAGAM is an experimental research and technological demonstration system developed for SIH 2026 PS 26081.**  
> It is **NOT** an official weather warning or disaster management bulletin system of the Government of India or the India Meteorological Department (IMD).

- **No Legal or Emergency Authority:** Forecasts, risk scores, and threshold alerts generated by AAGAM must not be used as the sole basis for life-safety, civil aviation, maritime evacuation, or disaster response decisions.
- **Authoritative Source:** For official, legally binding severe weather warnings, cyclone alerts, and flood advisories, operators and citizens must refer exclusively to the [India Meteorological Department](https://mausam.imd.gov.in) and the [National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA)](https://ndma.gov.in).

## 3. Scientific Terminology: "Assimilation" Clarification

- **AI-Based Statistical Blending vs. Dynamical Data Assimilation (DA):**  
  In classical physical meteorology, *Data Assimilation* refers to mathematical variational methods (e.g., 3D-Var, 4D-Var) or Ensemble Kalman Filtering (EnKF) that inject raw observation radiances, radiosonde profiles, and Doppler weather radar data into the Navier-Stokes primitive equations before running a numerical integration.
- **AAGAM Methodology:**  
  AAGAM performs **AI-Grid Statistical Multi-Model Post-Processing and Adaptive Weight Assimilation**. It takes post-integration surface point forecast fields from 4 independent global forecasting engines (ECMWF IFS, NOAA GFS, DWD ICON, ECMWF AIFS), conditions them against regional observational climatology, and dynamically optimizes an ensemble blending matrix using regularized machine learning (Ridge Regression and LightGBM).

## 4. Input Data Latency & Observation Constraints

- **Observation Publication Lag:**  
  High-resolution IMD gridded observation datasets and ground station records typically undergo 24–48 hour quality-control validation prior to public release. In real-time operational cycles, AAGAM uses the latest verified ground observations and reanalysis baselines for bias correction.
- **Open-Meteo API Dependency:**  
  Base NWP model feeds are sourced through the Open-Meteo Seamless API. If upstream model runs (e.g., 00Z GFS or 12Z ECMWF) experience distribution delays at NOAA or ECMWF, AAGAM's ingestion pipeline flags the run, logs the latency, and falls back to the previous model cycle while raising a degraded advisory flag.

## 5. Free-Tier Cloud Hosting & Infrastructure Constraints

The public demonstration of AAGAM is hosted on cloud free/developer tier services, which impose specific operational characteristics:

1. **Render Web Service (FastAPI Backend):**
   - **Cold Start Delay:** If idle for more than 15 minutes, the container spins down. The initial incoming request incurs a 30–50 second cold-start spin-up delay. Subsequent warm requests respond in $< 500$ ms.
   - **Remediation:** Self-ping cron workflows and client-side loading spinners are configured to mitigate cold starts during scheduled demonstrations.

2. **Supabase PostgreSQL (Database & Auth):**
   - **Storage Limit:** 500 MB database storage limit. Automated retention policies purge raw non-00Z model forecasts older than 90 days after archiving them into nightly Parquet backups.
   - **Inactivity Pause:** Supabase automatically pauses projects after 7 days of zero database queries. Pre-demo verification checks ensure the project is active.
   - **Connection Pool:** Transaction pooler (PgBouncer) is capped at 15 concurrent pooled connections; `statement_cache_size=0` is required.

3. **Groq Cloud API (AI Assistant):**
   - **Developer Tier Rate Limits:** 30 Requests Per Minute (RPM) and 6,000 Tokens Per Minute (TPM) for `llama-3.3-70b-versatile`.
   - **Remediation:** In-memory query caching, strict token budgeting (max 1,024 completion tokens), and an explicit HTTP 429 graceful fallback drawer alert are implemented.

## 6. Model Generalization Boundaries

- **Variables Covered:**  
  Currently optimized for three core operational surface variables:
  1. 24-hour Accumulated Precipitation (`rain_mm`)
  2. Maximum Surface Air Temperature (`tmax_c`)
  3. Maximum Wind Gust / Speed (`wind_max_kmh`)
- **Variables Not Covered:**  
  Atmospheric soundings, dew point depression, convective available potential energy (CAPE), sea surface temperature, and tropical cyclone track vortex tracking are outside the current demonstration scope.
- **Lead-Time Horizon:**  
  Optimized for Day 1 through Day 7 forecasts. Skill score degradation beyond Day 7 approaches climatological baselines and is excluded from active alert triggering.
